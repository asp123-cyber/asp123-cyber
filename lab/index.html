Why do the fidelity and Fourier models agree?

The encoding prepares |ψ(x)⟩ = Rᵧ(mx)|0⟩, where m is the winding. Its squared overlap is K(x,z) = cos²[m(x−z)/2]. The same value is the ordinary dot product of the three features (1, cos(mx), sin(mx))/√2.

Both models use ridge regression with the same penalty. The simulator fits the qubit model from its Gram matrix and fits the classical model independently from its three features. Their largest score difference on this test set is .

Data, training and limits

Labels are given by sign(cos(2x)), with angles in radians. Training uses 64 angles and testing uses 48, from separately jittered regular grids spanning −π to π. Fixed seeds are 31415 and 27182. All three readouts use λ = 0.35 and predict class +1 for a score of zero or higher. The raw-angle baseline uses features (1, x/π).

The literal factor of two in the labels is intentional. A 2× rotation supplies the matching harmonic. The other encodings show what happens when the feature map misses it. These observations concern this synthetic dataset and ideal statevectors.
